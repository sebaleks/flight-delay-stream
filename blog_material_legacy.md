## Flagship narrative (chapters 1–3)

### Chapter 1 - The Refuted Pre-Registered Hypothesis (cascade features lift mornings, not evenings)
- **Numbers / evidence:** Pre-registered baseline (hourly-weather generation): XGB **ROC 0.6979 / PR-AUC 0.3893**; evening lift **1.71x**, morning lift **1.94x**. Declared pass condition (written verbatim before any code): **Delta-lift(evening) > Delta-lift(morning)**. Result **REFUTED** on both models. Contaminated cascade model (PR #16): Morning 5-11 lift **1.94x -> 2.66x (Delta-lift +0.72)**; Evening 17-23 lift **1.71x -> 1.98x (Delta-lift +0.27)** — mornings gained ~3x more. Restricted production model (PR #18, verdict held): morning **1.94x -> 2.56x (Delta +0.62)**; evening **1.71x -> 1.95x (Delta +0.24)**. Morning/evening lift across three generations: daily prior-day **1.61x / 1.53x**; hourly at-departure **1.94x / 1.71x**; cascade restricted (shipping) **2.56x / 1.95x**. Both slices' absolute ROC improved (contaminated): Morning ROC **0.6645 -> 0.7087**, Evening ROC **0.6830 -> 0.7248**. Mechanism evidence: monotone position-risk **14.2% at leg 1 -> 29.4% at leg 6+** (training window); face-validity turnaround gradient **13.9% at >=2h slack -> 30.3% under 35 min**. Overall headline PR-AUC uplift +0.0855 (+22.0% rel.), ROC +0.0418. Naive marginal pattern that made the hypothesis plausible: dashboard evening peak **30.0% delay at hour 20:00, ~3.4x the 05:00 low** (PR #12).
- **Why it's strong:** "I wrote down a falsifiable prediction before coding, it failed, and I published the failure plus the real mechanism instead of quietly deleting it."
- **Narrative beat:** The intuitive story (evenings are worst, so cascade features should help evenings most) is exactly backwards. The turn: features pay off where cascade EXPOSURE varies, not where cascade effects dominate — by evening nearly every aircraft is deep in rotation (exposure high but near-uniform, so a feature measuring it discriminates weakly), while mornings hold the full variance (leg-1-after-overnight vs a 06:00 on a tail that landed 01:30).
- **Source:** PR #16 (primary — hypothesis + contaminated result), PR #18 (refutation survives on the clean/restricted model), PR #14 (staleness counter-argument seeds the mechanism), code: rotation_model, PR #8/#12 (naive evening-peak marginal backdrop). Cross-ref Chapter 2 (same clean model) and Chapter 3 (same lift-correction discipline).
- **Details:**
  - The hypothesis was pre-registered verbatim in the PR body before any code, with an explicit declared pass condition (Delta-lift(evening) > Delta-lift(morning)), and tested against the lift-corrected slice table — "not explained after it." Reported REFUTED rather than dropped or rationalized.
  - The verdict held identical **before and after** the PR #16 overlap-rule fix (223k phantom successors) AND after the PR #18 tail-swap restriction — a ~3x margin either way, so the refutation is robust to both the data-quality fix and the leakage fix.
  - Lift-correction is the load-bearing methodology: "per-slice PR-AUC is meaningless without its base rate." The raw evening PR-AUC (0.424) vs morning PR-AUC (0.208) gap is PREVALENCE, not skill (evening base rate 0.277 vs morning 0.129); only lift-over-prevalence is comparable across slices. Corrected, lift is what shows mornings gaining ~3x more.
  - Post-hoc mechanism is explicitly LABELED as interpretation-after-the-fact, "not a rescue of the registration" — the registered test stands as refuted, and the new reading (within-slice variance of rotation position drives the morning gain) is flagged as itself testable-but-not-pre-registered.
  - Generalizable ML principle stated in prose: "features pay off where cascade exposure VARIES, not where cascades LIVE." The monotone position-risk curve (14.2% at leg 1 -> 29.4% at leg 6+) is the gradient the model exploits; mornings span the whole curve, evenings cluster at the deep end.
  - Staleness counter-argument (from PR #14, strengthens the mechanism): the arithmetic runs opposite to a naive staleness story — a 07:00 departure sits ~7h after the prior-day window closed, a 20:00 departure ~20h, so evenings actually had the STALER prior-day weather, yet mornings gained more. Offered mechanism: morning delays are weather-determined (little overnight disruption accumulated), evening delays are cascade-determined.
  - Retroactive explanation of the older daily model: its roughly-flat lift across the day is explained by its inability to exploit the weather-determined morning regime without time-resolved weather — the previous model's weakness becomes evidence for the new mechanism.
  - Face-validity was checked on raw training-window rates before trusting the model: monotone in turnaround band (13.9% at >=2h slack -> 30.3% under 35 min) and in rotation position (14.2% leg 1 -> 29.4% leg 6+).
  - Not CI-catchable: the entire chapter is a methodological/analytical result (a pre-registered prediction tested against a slice table), invisible to ruff/gitleaks/dbt-parse.
  - PINNED-NUMBER NOTE: chapter uses the restricted production morning/evening figures (2.56x / 1.95x) per the pinned description; the contaminated PR #16 figures (2.66x / 1.98x) are the same hypothesis on the pre-restriction model and should be labeled as such if both are cited.

### Chapter 2 - The Tail-Swap Leakage Audit (measured it instead of dismissing or deleting)
- **Numbers / evidence:** Three-way controlled comparison (XGB, identical row set 20,240,662 / split 16,678,880 train / 3,561,782 test / identical hyperparameters): **ROC** hourly baseline **0.6979** / cascade contaminated **0.7397** / restricted adopted **0.7389**; **PR-AUC (headline)** baseline **0.3893** / contaminated **0.4748** / restricted **0.4652**. **89% of the PR-AUC uplift survived the restriction: +0.0759 of +0.0855**; swap-linkage share ≈ **0.0096 (~11%)**; ROC kept **98%** of its cascade gain. Schedule-consistency classes (overall / evening 17-23): **a consistent inbound 91.95% / 92.65%; b clean first leg 3.93% / 2.50%; c swap-shaped 4.12% / 4.85%** (swap-shape overrepresented in evenings). Mechanism verified 3 ways: **no_inbound band training delay rate 0.388 -> 0.224** once swap rows leave; cleaned **turnaround-band feature rose to #2** classifier importance; **operated-chain rotation_position fell #4 -> #8**. Regression got slightly WORSE under restriction: RMSE/MAE **51.70/20.22 (baseline) -> 49.56/19.00 (cascade) -> 49.71/19.10 (restricted)**. Logreg **0.6550/0.3310 -> 0.6920/0.3998 -> 0.6654/0.3382** (restricted retains almost none of the uplift). 10-decimal pinned restricted headline **ROC 0.7388902208 / PR-AUC 0.4651540494** (matches pinned exactly). Fingerprint that started it: **no_inbound band 38.8% training delay rate vs 19.7% base rate** ("suspiciously hot for first leg of the day"). Tail-unknown legs **0.34%** (now class c). Duty window gap **[0, 840] minutes (0-14h)**. Guard is **13 arms**, passes in **7.3s**.
- **Why it's strong:** "A reviewer said my rotation feature could smuggle a same-day outcome. Instead of arguing or deleting it, I ran a clean ablation and measured exactly how much of my headline was leakage — 11% — and shipped the honest number."
- **Narrative beat:** The leakage rule is extended from feature VALUES to feature LINKAGE — the rotation features read only schedule columns, yet the graph edges themselves (chained by the post-hoc OPERATED tail) encoded a day-of outcome. A prior PR's honest "epistemology caveat" is deleted from the mart header and paid off as a production restriction. Costed decision: ship 0.4652 over the contaminated 0.4748, paying 0.0096 PR-AUC for honesty.
- **Source:** PR #18 (primary), PR #16 (origin of the disclosed caveat + Codex P1), PR #17 (serving reconciliation), code: rotation_model, serving_edges, CLAUDE.md §9. Cross-ref Chapter 1 (refutation lives on this clean model) and Chapter 3 (leakage discipline).
- **Details:**
  - Central methodological turn: rotation features chain legs by the post-hoc OPERATED tail; a swap-restructured linkage is itself a day-of operational outcome correlated with disruption. This RESOLVES a limitation a prior PR (#16) had only disclosed honestly — the old "EPISTEMOLOGY CAVEAT" block in the int_aircraft_rotation header was deleted and replaced with "THE TAIL-SWAP RESTRICTION."
  - Proxy under missing ground truth: BTS has no planned-rotation field, so **schedule-consistency is the leakage proxy** (a production system would use the airline's planned-rotation feed); the author states this explicitly rather than pretending the proxy is exact.
  - Clean ablation design: a variant mart nulled every rotation feature for class c and recomputed both hist grains over clean links only, with weather, route/carrier/airport history, cutoff, and split untouched and exact row+split parity, then a full retrain — so each delta is attributable to the linkage restriction alone.
  - Three-class taxonomy carried by one nullable boolean has_inbound_leg: TRUE = consistent inbound (a: gap [0,840]min, prior dest = this origin, prior not schedule-overlapped); FALSE = clean first leg (b: no prior, or overnight break >14h parked here); NULL = swap-shaped (c: negative gap, continuity violation, overlapped prior, unknown tail, prior with unknown scheduled arrival). Class c carries NULL for every rotation column INCLUDING band keys and is excluded from the shared rates.
  - Density KEPT for swap-shaped rows deliberately — it's a schedule aggregate (departures per origin-hour), not tail-based — a per-feature judgment about which columns actually depend on the operated-tail linkage.
  - Mechanism verified at the FEATURE level, not just the metric: no_inbound band's training rate fell 0.388 -> 0.224 (the elevated rate was substantially "a disruption already touched this aircraft today"), and the retrained model reorganized exactly as a real-signal hypothesis predicts — cleaned turnaround-band rose to #2 importance while operated-chain rotation_position (which absorbs swap restructuring) fell #4 -> #8.
  - Honest failure disclosed: the linear logreg baseline retains almost none of the uplift under the restriction (0.3382 vs 0.3310 hourly) because it median-imputes the 4.12% all-NULL rows and loses the band separation; the surviving signal is largely tree-accessible (XGBoost consumes NULLs natively). This candor partly concedes the Codex P1 that the tree's edge here is the missing-value branch.
  - Regression honesty: RMSE/MAE got marginally WORSE under the restriction (49.71/19.10 vs 49.56/19.00 contaminated) — shipped and reported anyway rather than cherry-picking the classifier win.
  - Truth-in-headline discipline: the experiment's originally-approved headline was 0.7390 / 0.4653; a review-driven ~6-row edge refinement moved it to 0.7389 / 0.4652 (inside the ±0.002 noise band). The shift was flagged for the approver rather than quietly swapped; every doc pins the corrected number; the contaminated 0.7397 / 0.4748 survive only as labeled comparison context. The edge refinement affects ~6 rows in 20.7M but perturbs a band-rate feature's low-order bits on ~1.2M rows.
  - Determinism: the production rebuild reproduced the experiment run BYTE-IDENTICALLY through an entirely DIFFERENT construction path (a passthrough-patch variant table vs a full dbt rebuild), plus a second rebuild+retrain; the final ~6-row refinement got its own rebuild-pair (two more full rebuild+retrains), byte-identical at 10 decimals. Framed as an EMPIRICAL rebuild-stability result, NOT a BigQuery contract about distributed aggregation order; pre-refinement contaminated pin ROC 0.7396687332 / PR-AUC 0.4748073992.
  - The guard as executable epistemology: a 13-arm dbt assertion that independently re-derives the three schedule-consistency classes from silver crs columns and pins BOTH directions (swap rows fully NULL, clean rows fully carry). Caught by review, not CI. Converse-hole insight: constancy arms that count DISTINCT non-null values silently pass an all-NULL band as "0 distinct," and identity arms' inner joins silently drop an entity row that vanished from the rates model — so a whole class of regressions slips past unless a converse arm pins the positive direction. Two independent reviewers (Codex + adversarial workflow) converged on this converse hole (fix commit 2392189). Meta-honesty: the PR body "said 13 [arms] when it was 12; miscount, corrected by this addition."
  - The overrepresentation of swap-shape in evenings (4.85% evening vs 4.12% overall) independently confirmed the accumulation pattern the leakage concern had predicted — a checkable side-prediction.
  - Unresolved-automated-objection tension: a GitHub Codex bot P1 leakage objection (start_line 194, line 197 on int_aircraft_rotation.sql) was posted ~2 min after PR open, got 0 reactions, was never replied to, and stood when the PR merged ~3 hours later — the substantive counter-argument lives only in the PR body and PR #17's serving contract. A genuine blog angle: when is it defensible to merge over an unrebutted automated reviewer, and the subtle distinction between removing a leak's VALUE versus its missingness INDICATOR.
  - Serving reconciliation (deferred to PR #17): all-NULL rotation is now IN-distribution but semantically MEANS "operated linkage was swap-restructured" (a day-of outcome), distinct from a merely-unknown future plan at serve time; PR #17 needed no code change because band reconstruction already excludes NULL-position rows and the typical-profile fallback filters on has_inbound_leg.
  - Deliberate NON-action: three measured guard-hardening gaps were documented and deferred to a standalone PR to keep this PR scoped to the restriction.
  - Second-round adversarial workflow: 20/26 agents completed; 6 limit-killed verifications triaged directly. PR size 320 additions / 146 deletions, 13 files. Reviewed commit dd09670, converse-fix 2392189, merge 26b858b.
  - Related overlap fix (from PR #16, the data-quality precursor): 341k same-tail overlapping scheduled intervals (2/3 overlap by >30 min — phantom/duplicate records), whose 223k successor rows would have served as "valid" inbounds; fail-closing them IMPROVED every metric (ROC 0.7339 -> 0.7397, PR-AUC 0.4624 -> 0.4748) — a data-quality fix that also happened to be a metric win, the opposite of the usual fix-costs-accuracy tradeoff.

### Chapter 3 - LOO -> Tree-Exploitable Channel -> Smoothed Target Encoding (FLAGSHIP)
- **Numbers / evidence:** First training run of the LOO (v2) mart: boosters WORSE than logistic regression — **XGB ROC-AUC 0.559**; regressor RMSE worse than predicting the train mean. The diagnostic TELL: **XGBoost beat logreg by only +0.007 ROC on 20.24M tabular rows** (anomalous — GBDTs should decisively beat linear at that scale). After the v3 smoothed fix: **XGB-over-logreg +0.007 -> +0.035 ROC**; **PR-AUC lift XGB/logreg 1.53x -> 1.77x**; **feature importances 7 (carrier-only fixation) -> 36 of 37 features, led by route history**. v2 LOO formula removed: `hist_rate = (n_raw*rate_raw - cast(arr_del15 as int64))/(n_raw-1)`. v3 shipped formula: **hist_* = (n·p + m·global)/(n + m), m = 50**. Residual: own-label weight **1/(n+m) ≤ 1/51 ≈ 0.196**; **~102 of 16,678,880** single-flight-route training rows collapse to **0.206 vs 0.226**; **entity self-share via the prior bounded ≤ 3.0e-6** at every grain (N=16.7M). m-sensitivity (full retrains): m∈{10,50,100} -> **XGB ROC 0.6806/0.6809/0.6805, PR-AUC 0.3478/0.3482/0.3476** (deltas < 0.001 across a 10x range). Split **16,678,880 train / 3,561,782 test / 0 overlap**, boundary **2024-06-30 -> 2024-07-01**, total **20,240,662**. Held-out test base rate **19.69% (0.1969)**. Shipped v3 model (daily-weather generation): majority-class baseline Acc **0.8031** / ROC **0.500** / PR-AUC **0.1969**; logreg Acc **0.6325** / ROC **0.6464** / PR-AUC **0.3017** (1.53x); XGB Acc **0.6778** / ROC **0.6809** / PR-AUC **0.3482** (1.77x). Regressor: predict-mean RMSE **52.68** / MAE **22.46**; XGB RMSE **52.08** / MAE **20.66**. Regression error tail-dominated: MAE **~13 min (on-time) -> ~315 min (3h+ delays)**. PR #10, commit **0b717b7**, merged 2026-07-15.
- **Why it's strong:** "A textbook-correct leave-one-out target encoding was silently crippling my gradient boosters. The tell wasn't a too-good-to-be-true score — it was the model being anomalously WORSE than it should be. I traced an invertible per-row label channel, confirmed it three independent ways, and designed it out of existence."
- **Narrative beat:** The inverse of the classic leakage story. Leakage almost always makes a model look too good; this artifact made it look BAD (XGB 0.559, worse than logreg). Crucially it DEGRADED quality but NEVER inflated any reported metric — because the split isolates labels, test features never contained test labels; every reported number was honest throughout. And the "fix" that created it (LOO removing a 102-row static leak) manufactured a far worse 16.7M-row distributional one — the cure was worse than the disease.
- **Source:** PR #10 (primary — pipeline built + diagnosed + fixed), PR #5 (FLAGSHIP SEED — LOO born, the 102-row measurement, dest-LOO Codex P1), code: ml_targetencoding, determinism_shared, PR #11 (determinism/serving follow-up). Cross-ref Chapter 2 (leakage discipline), Chapter 1 (lift-correction).
- **Details:**
  - The three-version arc (preserved in the mart SQL header so the reasoning travels with the code): **v1** raw rates — a single-flight route's whole-window hist rate EQUALED its own label exactly (feature == label), measured as **102 single-flight routes** and **120/120 two-flight routes** where the LOO value equals exactly the peer's outcome (PR #5). **v2** leave-one-out — algebraically removes a row's own contribution `((n·rate - label)/(n-1))` without a second aggregate query, so the rate definition still lives once; fixed the 102-row static leak but opened the tree channel. **v3** smoothed raw rate — `(n·p + m·global)/(n+m)`, m=50, CONSTANT within an entity (identical for every row, train and test), so no per-row channel can exist BY CONSTRUCTION.
  - Why LOO is tree-exploitable but not linear-exploitable: LOO writes each training row's feature as a deterministic per-row function of that row's own label; a linear model can only use the entity-level mean of the column, but a gradient-boosted tree can carve fine enough splits to memorize the micro-perturbation — an INVERTIBLE trace of the label present in train and absent in test, so it HURTS generalization while looking like "signal" in importances.
  - The diagnostic anomaly: XGB beating logreg by only +0.007 ROC on 20.24M rows (first run XGB actually WORSE at 0.559) is the tell — a gap that small is impossible for a healthy GBDT at that scale.
  - Root cause confirmed THREE independent ways (a reusable diagnostic template): (1) sklearn HistGradientBoosting collapsed identically -> rules out an XGBoost library bug; (2) the broken importances sat exactly on the large-n carrier features (where per-row LOO perturbations are most memorizable); (3) quantizing the features alone revived the booster on an identical sample -> proves the channel lived in the low-order bits.
  - CRITICAL honesty distinction, stated plainly in the mart header and README: the artifact DEGRADED the model but NEVER inflated metrics — test features never contained test labels; every reported number was honest throughout. It was not a leak in the too-good-to-be-true sense; the inverse signature (worse-than-it-should-be) is what surfaced it.
  - The fix is textbook Bayesian shrinkage / smoothed target encoding; because the value is constant within an entity, the pathology is structurally impossible rather than algebraically cancelled — strictly better than LOO, which only removes self-inclusion at the cost of injecting the per-row trace. Intuition pinned in the header: below ~50 observations an entity rate is noise-dominated and should shrink hard toward the prior.
  - m=50 justified by SENSITIVITY, not tuned to test: across m∈{10,50,100} XGB ROC is 0.6806/0.6809/0.6805 and PR-AUC 0.3478/0.3482/0.3476 (deltas < 0.001), so the choice is defensibly arbitrary and shrinkage only bites at tiny n.
  - Residual accounting is quantified, not hand-waved: after smoothing, ~102 single-flight-route training rows still encode their own label at 1/51 weight (feature takes (50g)/51 ≈ 0.206 or (1+50g)/51 ≈ 0.226 at n=1), 102 of 16,678,880 rows, no test-side effect, entity self-share via the prior bounded ≤ 3.0e-6 — accepted by explicit owner decision.
  - The bug was discovered by SHIPPING, not by review or CI: two commits, one story each — build the pipeline (commit 1415268), watch the boosters come out worse than logreg, deep-dive, fix the mart (commit 0b717b7). The pre-training leakage audit (37 features, zero label_/forbidden columns, live mart schema == audited 43-column allowlist exactly) is a HARD GATE but STRUCTURALLY CANNOT see a distributional per-row channel like LOO; credential-free CI (dbt parse + ruff + gitleaks + Dagster definitions-load — never trains, never runs dbt tests) would pass a mart that cripples the model. This is a distinct catch-mechanism from the rest of the project: model diagnosis, not adversarial review, not CI.

### Chapter 5 - The error ceiling ("the modest number is a choice")

- **Numbers / evidence:**
  - Regression MAE by true-delay bucket: **~13 min (on-time) rising to ~315 min (3h+ delays)** (PR #10).
  - Cascade generation on the tail (PR #16): 60-180 min bucket MAE 76.8 → 68.7; **180+ min bucket MAE 310.8 → 281.0** (still ~5 hours of error).
  - Regressor vs predict-train-mean baseline (PR #10): XGBoost RMSE 52.08 / MAE 20.66 vs mean-predictor RMSE 52.68 / MAE 22.46 — barely beats the mean on RMSE (tail-dominated) but clearly on MAE.
  - Shipped tuned regressor: RMSE 49.264351 / MAE 18.985849 (49.26 / 18.99). Held-out classifier headline: ROC 0.7388902208 / PR-AUC 0.4651540494 (0.7389 / 0.4652); base rate 0.1969.
  - `p90 arr delay` computed via `approx_quantiles(arr_delay_minutes, 100)[offset(90)]` (PR #5 delay_measures macro) — foreshadows the tail framing.
- **Why it's strong:** Names an honest, defensible upper bound and argues that published AUCs far above 0.73 on pre-departure-only features likely leak `dep_delay` or same-day weather.
- **Narrative beat:** The RMSE-vs-MAE split is the tell: XGBoost barely beats the mean on RMSE (52.08 vs 52.68) yet clearly beats it on MAE (20.66 vs 22.46), because RMSE is dominated by a tail (3h+ delays, MAE ~315 min) that is day-of-unpredictable, while MAE rewards nailing the on-time mass. "The artifact only degraded the model, it never inflated metrics" (Ch 3/Ch 9) is the mirror-image honesty beat.
- **Source:** PR #10 (ML deep dive), PR #16 (cascade tail), PR #19 (tuning near-null); code: `ml_targetencoding`.
- **Details:**
  - Mechanism: extreme delays are day-of phenomena (mechanical AOG, ATC ground stops, crew timeouts) that no pre-departure feature can see; the ceiling is acknowledged as **irreducible for this regime** rather than papered over. The leakage rule (CLAUDE.md §9) forbids `DepDelay`, `ArrDelay`, actual gate/wheels times, same-flight realized weather — the very columns that would break the ceiling.
  - **Prevalence-vs-skill correction** (PR #10): per-slice PR-AUC is meaningless without its base rate. The apparent evening/morning PR-AUC gap (evening 0.424 vs morning 0.208) is PREVALENCE, not skill — lift is roughly flat (evening 1.53× vs morning 1.61×). Slice table (daily era): route_hist_present base 0.197 / PR-AUC 0.348 / lift 1.77× / ROC 0.681; route_hist_missing base 0.228 / PR-AUC 0.307 / lift 1.35× / ROC 0.625; evening_dep_17_23 base 0.277 / PR-AUC 0.424 / lift 1.53× / ROC 0.660; morning_dep_5_11 base 0.129 / PR-AUC 0.208 / lift 1.61× / ROC 0.644. The claim that survives correction: **new/unseen routes genuinely degrade the model** (lift 1.35× vs 1.77× present).
  - **Tuning corroborates the ceiling** (Ch 7): Stage-3 hyperparameter search barely moves an already-strong model (regressor −0.4486 RMSE, classifier gets worse), reported straight — evidence that even tuning cannot dent errors driven by unknowable day-of shocks.
  - Classifier headline framing: with a ~1-in-5 base rate, accuracy is near-meaningless; the class-weighted XGBoost DELIBERATELY sacrifices accuracy (0.6778 < the do-nothing majority baseline 0.8031) to buy recall — which is why the majority baseline (ROC 0.500, PR-AUC 0.1969) is printed beside it and PR-AUC is the headline.

### Chapter 6 - Two train/serve mismatches, one closed

- **Numbers / evidence:**
  - Hourly-era held-out classifier metrics stand as reported because they are computed entirely on OBSERVED data: **ROC 0.6979 / PR-AUC 0.3893** (the remaining forecast-vs-observed gap never touches held-out metrics). Current production headline (restricted): ROC 0.7388902208 / PR-AUC 0.4651540494.
  - **ORD −6.5 h negative control** (PR #14): a naive unconverted UTC-vs-local join picks weather from the wrong hour, reaching 6.5 hours PAST scheduled departure — proving the seed-tz conversion is load-bearing.
  - Live forecast run: AA ORD→ATL 2026-07-31 17:30 (5 days out) → p(delay) 0.9534, E[min] 142.2 (NDFD forecast thunder; `had_thunder=1`) vs a clear-day run of the same route at 0.7824 / 44.6 min. Thunder is the #2 regressor feature.
  - NDFD horizon ~7 days; grid cache TTLs `GRID_TTL_S=1800` (30 min success) / `GRID_FAIL_TTL_S=300` (5 min failure).
- **Why it's strong:** Cleanly separates a mismatch you can engineer away (temporal misalignment) from one that is irreducible for ANY forecasting system (forecast ≠ observed), and closes the first without inflating any reported number.
- **Narrative beat:** The daily-era model carried BOTH a forecast-vs-observed gap AND a same-day-vs-prior-day temporal misalignment (daily GSOD had to be joined on `flight_date − 1` to avoid leakage). The hourly rebuild (training on the last observation at-or-before scheduled departure) plus forecast serving (NDFD value valid AT that same UTC hour, same tz conversion the mart uses) **collapses two mismatches into one** — the temporal one is eliminated; only forecast-vs-observed remains, and it cannot touch held-out metrics.
- **Source:** PR #14 (hourly rebuild), PR #15 (forecast-driven inference, Step 5), PR #17 (honest "one gap → two gaps" reframe); code: `serving_edges`.
- **Details:**
  - **Leak-free by construction:** a forecast issued now for a flight that has not departed predates departure, so it is legitimately pre-departure information (CLAUDE.md §9). `FlightRequest` carries schedule fields only.
  - **Symmetry of failure and missingness:** any forecast failure (off-grid point, beyond ~7-day horizon, API error, unresolved hour, unknown airport, NULL tz) lands on the EXACT training NULL path (all weather NaN + `has_origin_weather=0`). XGBoost consumes NaN natively; logreg imputes train medians. The failure mode is engineered to be in-distribution, not novel.
  - Three schema gates fail closed (PR #15): both XGB boosters' stored `feature_names` == canonical registry at load; logreg pipeline `feature_names_in_` == its contract; assembled frame checked against both before any prediction.
  - **Honest evolution of the accounting** (PR #17): docs moved from "now ONE gap" to explicitly **TWO gaps** — the (closed) weather forecast-vs-observed gap PLUS a NEW rotation/density serving-estimation gap that arrived with cascade features. Two separate response flags expose it: `rotation_context` (provided|typical_estimate) and `origin_density_source` (provided|estimated). The original temporal mismatch stayed closed; the second "gap" in the reframe is a different animal (estimating an unknown future plan), not the same-day/prior-day misalignment.
  - **The visibility fix is a live-test-changed-a-design-decision moment** (PR #15): NDFD publishes visibility mainly during restriction, so a clear-sky hour returns no value; leaving it NaN was a serving-only pattern → set absent-at-resolved-hour to 10.0 (the mart's right-censored clear-day value), which moved a clear-day ORD score 0.8069 → 0.7824.
  - **Freezing-precip train/serve encoding parity trap** (PR #14 SQL + PR #15 serving): the FRSHTT convention (freezing liquid precip counts as rain; AW 47-48, MW 56-57/66-67 → `had_rain_drizzle`; solid AW 45-46 → snow) was implemented WRONG independently in the silver decoder and again in the serving encoder, caught by two different reviewers — a shared convention duplicated across train and serve drifts on both sides. Neither was CI-catchable.
  - **np.float32 NaN-leak** (`serving_edges`, PR #15): the NULL-path guard used `isinstance(v, float)`, but assembled columns are `np.float32` (not Python `float`), so the check never fired and raw NaN leaked into JSON on the least-tested path; fixed with `pd.isna`. Caught by adversarial/Codex review, invisible to CI.
  - Byte-exact feature parity without formula duplication: hist_* rates read straight from `ml_flight_features` via `ANY_VALUE` (constant-within-entity), so serving reproduces training values bit-for-bit (cross-ref Ch 9, Ch 17).
  - Related self-contained-artifact work (PR #11): logreg preprocessing collapsed into ONE Pipeline (no drift surface); xgboost pinned >=3.0 because it persists categorical level mappings and re-codes by NAME (verified: 130,354/130,354 predictions reproduced, max abs diff 0.0, slice-local 5,481 route levels vs 7,539 trained) — under 2.x a differing category set mis-scored silently.

### Chapter 8 - Cross-validation reflection

- **Numbers / evidence:**
  - Time-based split via `is_training_row` (never random): train 16,678,880 / test 3,561,782, 0 overlap; boundary exactly **2024-06-30 → 2024-07-01** (`train_test_cutoff_date = 2024-07-01`), test ~last 6 months ≈ 17% (82.4 / 17.6).   - Single-slice validation optimism, quantified: validation slice 2024-05-06..2024-06-30 delay rate **0.260** vs test **0.197**; val PR-AUC ~0.515 vs test ~0.465; the classifier won validation by +0.0025 PR-AUC yet regressed on test (Δ −0.00056) — the concrete illustration.
  - Cutoff "bites" (not decorative): LAX-JFK pre-cutoff rate **0.2207977207977208** matches an independent recompute to full precision while the full-period rate is **0.2088673…** (PR #5).
- **Why it's strong:** Explains why the correct choice for the FINAL estimate (avoid random CV to prevent temporal leakage) is different from what would have made TUNING more robust (time-series CV) — and shows exactly how single-slice validation misled the classifier.
- **Narrative beat:** The project's own shared-rate design forces the CV subtlety into the open: because hist_* rates aggregate the whole pre-cutoff window, ANY validation slice carved from the training window inherits its own peers' outcomes — so naive in-window CV leaks time. This is precisely the Codex P1 the tuning PR argued over (Ch 7).
- **Source:** PR #5 (shared-model CV caveat), PR #10 (recorded caveat), PR #19 (single-slice non-transfer + the hist_* validation-leak debate).
- **Details:**
  - **Random CV is correctly avoided for the final estimate:** a random split would leak time and violate the leakage boundary (CLAUDE.md §9); the split is derived from the single cutoff var, never shuffled, and asserted at runtime.
  - **Documented residual honesty** (PR #5, PR #10): training-row rates still aggregate the WHOLE pre-cutoff window — a 2022 row sees 2023 peers' outcomes. Accepted because the shared-model requirement (one rate definition feeding both dashboard and ML) rules out per-flight as-of-date rates. Stated fix for anyone doing validation: "anyone carving a validation slice from the training window must re-derive rates as-of that slice."
  - **Time-series CV would have made tuning more robust:** the classifier's non-transfer (val win, test loss) is the textbook single-slice optimism symptom; multiple temporal folds would have averaged out the summer slice's high base rate (0.260 vs 0.197) that inflated absolute val PR-AUC. Only RELATIVE ranking within a split is meaningful.
  - The Codex P1 in PR #19 recommended materializing fit-only historical rates as of `VAL_START` — the "re-derive rates as-of the slice" fix — declined on the grounds that shorter-window rates would optimize hyperparameters for a distribution the shipped full-window model never sees, and that re-deriving in Python breaks the SQL-only gold rule. A genuine methodological tension worth surfacing: doing proper time-series CV here would require re-deriving the target encoding per fold.
  - Guardrail proof the cutoff fires: LAX-JFK 0.2207977207977208 (pre-cutoff) vs 0.2088673… (full period) — the boundary demonstrably changes the number.

  - **Bit-identical headline through a FULL Dagster mart rebuild + retrain:** ROC 0.6806027430 / PR-AUC 0.3478668781 held to all TEN decimals (daily-era, PR #13); production restricted headline pinned at **ROC 0.7388902208 / PR-AUC 0.4651540494**, byte-identical across two more independent full rebuild+retrains and across an entirely DIFFERENT construction path (passthrough-patch variant table vs full dbt rebuild) (PR #18).   - **Logreg 7e-5 drift diagnosed:** ~7×10⁻⁵ refit drift = the lbfgs noise floor; lbfgs terminates on tolerance at n_iter **31-40 of 200** (never the cap, path-dependent); tightening tol to 1e-8 still leaves ~3×10⁻⁵ float32-vs-float64 + column-ordering residual. The ddof-0-vs-1 hypothesis **REFUTED**: rescale = **1 + 3×10⁻⁸** at n=16.7M — off by ~3 orders of magnitude. House rule: compare metrics at 4 decimals; sub-1e-4 is meaningless.
  - Cost of determinism: one in-memory sort, ~30 s per run. Loader column count `len(FEATURES)+4` → `+6`.
  - Calibration artifact determinism (PR #20): Platt a=1.1060015584787515, b=−1.2371760469682864 bit-identical; calibration blocks of two runs (20260730_100558, 20260730_101925) diff byte-for-byte identical; pure-numpy artifact, `np.interp` reproduces `IsotonicRegression.predict` to atol=1e-9.
- **Why it's strong:** Bit-identical ML metrics through a full warehouse rebuild is uncommon; the mechanism (histogram binning absorbs ULP-level float-aggregation noise) is non-obvious and reusable.
- **Narrative beat:** The honesty beat — a seductive wrong root cause (sklearn StandardScaler's ddof=0 population std) was killed by writing out the arithmetic (1 + 3e-8 vs an observed 7e-5, three orders of magnitude off) and tracing the real cause to lbfgs tolerance termination. And the epistemic arc: the claim was first stated as "deterministic GIVEN A FIXED MART BUILD" with an explicit refusal to over-claim across rebuilds, then UPGRADED to "verified across a full rebuild" only once Dagster could drive one — the ml/data.py comment was literally edited from "has not been verified" to "verified ACROSS a full mart rebuild."
- **Source:** PR #11 (canonical sort + ddof refutation + xgboost pin), PR #13 (Dagster across-rebuild upgrade), PR #14 / PR #16 / PR #18 (rebuild-pair verifications), PR #20 (calibration determinism); code: `determinism_shared`, `calibration_internals`.
- **Details:**
  - **Root cause:** the BigQuery Storage API returns rows in nondeterministic order across reads, and XGBoost `tree_method='hist'` training is row-order sensitive → ~0.001-0.002 cross-run spread. Because five fits on one loaded frame were already bit-identical, the variance had to be read order, not the estimator.
  - **Fix = canonical sort on the mart's tested-unique flight grain:** the FULL six-column key `(flight_date, carrier, flight_number, origin, dest, crs_dep_time)`, `ignore_index=True`. The intuitive four-column subset `(flight_date, carrier, origin, dest)` is NOT guaranteed tie-free (a carrier can fly the same origin-dest twice a day), so `flight_number` and `crs_dep_time` (cast to string) are added as sort-key-only bookkeeping columns, never model inputs. This is exactly the grain `int_historical_delay_rates` and the mart's `QUALIFY row_number()` partition use. Verified: two fully independent load+fit runs identical to 10 decimals (ROC 0.6806027430 / PR-AUC 0.3478668781).
  - **Histogram-binning mechanism (the Ch 9 punchline):** XGBoost `hist` buckets continuous feature values, so a ULP-level change in a hist_* rate from a different distributed float-summation order lands in the same bin and produces an identical split. The sort kills read-order nondeterminism; the binning absorbs aggregation-order nondeterminism. A performance choice (`hist` for speed) doubling as a reproducibility guarantee.
  - **Determinism chain across layers** (PR #14): deterministic silver dedup (METAR-first report-type preference + `farm_fingerprint` total-order tiebreak over every raw field) → deterministic mart `QUALIFY` on a uniqueness-tested natural key → canonical six-column sort at load. `metrics.json` byte-identical across 3 full training runs (run 3 after final SQL).
  - **Claim upgrade** (PR #13): two-stage test — materialize `ml_training` alone (bit-identical) then a full `monthly_refresh` that rebuilt the mart via dbt and retrained (same numbers to all ten decimals). Framed explicitly as an EMPIRICAL result, NOT a BigQuery contract about distributed aggregation order; a future last-bit shift would move metrics only within the historical **±0.002** band, immediately visible against the pinned headline. The 10-digit pin IS the regression test.
  - **xgboost >=3 categorical contract** (PR #11): under 2.x the UBJ artifact did not persist categorical level mappings, so a serving frame with a different category set mis-scored SILENTLY; verified in a fresh process (slice-local 5,481 route levels vs 7,539 trained, reloaded model reproduced 130,354/130,354 predictions, max abs diff 0.0) then pin raised 2.1 → 3.0 with a do-not-relax warning. "Verify-then-pin," pinning for behavior not API.
  - **m-sensitivity as a determinism/robustness check** (`determinism_shared`): smoothing prior m=50; across m ∈ {10,50,100} XGB ROC 0.6806/0.6809/0.6805 and PR-AUC 0.3478/0.3482/0.3476 (deltas < 0.001).
  - **Calibration determinism** (PR #20): the persisted `Calibrator` is pure numpy (two Platt scalars + isotonic threshold arrays, NO sklearn object pickled) → version-independent and byte-deterministic; a fit-time guard asserts `np.interp` reproduces `IsotonicRegression.predict` to atol=1e-9 so a future sklearn threshold-semantics change fails loudly.
  - **All caught by review/measurement, never CI:** the six-column sort, the ddof refutation, the xgboost >=3 pin, and the np.float32/pd.isna NaN leak were human/Codex catches. CI (gitleaks + offline dbt parse + dagster definitions validate + ruff) never trains, never runs dbt tests, and opens no cloud connection — the byte-reproducibility that would let CI catch a regression exists, but the gate that checks it is orchestrated/manual, not the PR CI.

### Chapter 11 - The GSOD pressure decode (wrong on ~45% of rows while passing every test)
- **Numbers / evidence:** Without the decode, **~45% of usable pressure values** would have entered silver as silent garbage. GSOD stores station pressure ≥1000 mb with the leading '1' dropped (stored `13.5` = **1013.5 mb**). The de-facto missing marker is **999.9**, NOT the documented **9999.9** (which never occurs in 2022-2024). Decode rule: `CASE WHEN stp IN (999.9, 9999.9) THEN NULL; WHEN stp < 100 THEN stp + 1000; ELSE stp END`. Nothing occurs in **[100, 500)**, so the +1000 reversal is unambiguous. Decoded values span a physical **706-1055 mb**. `accepted_range` on `mean_station_pressure_mb`: min 450 / max 1090, `severity: warn`. GSOD sentinels nulled: 9999.9 / 999.9 / 99.99. Staging: 12.0M global GSOD rows → 1,261,017 silver_weather rows (relevance filter to 1,165 stations nearest the 1,274 airports).
- **Why it's strong:** A single, concrete "trust the data, not the docs" defect that would have quietly corrupted a physical feature on nearly half of rows — and no test in the repo could see it.
- **Narrative beat:** The NOAA documentation says 9999.9 is the missing marker; empirically 9999.9 *never appears* and 999.9 is the live one, while ≥1000 mb values are truncated — so the "by the book" fix (`nullif(stp, 9999.9)`) leaves ~45% of rows wrong. Verified against the actual 2022-2024 distribution, not the spec.
- **Source:** PR #3 (feat/silver-staging); code area silver_weather.
- **Details:**
  - **The two-part quirk:** (1) leading-digit truncation for ≥1000 mb (13.5 → 1013.5); (2) 999.9 used as a live missing marker inside the plausible-looking range. A naive ingest passes both truncated two-digit values and 999.9 markers through as real millibar readings.
  - **Why the decode is unambiguous:** the empirical distribution has a *gap* — nothing falls in [100, 500) over 2022-2024 — so `stp < 100 → +1000` cannot mis-fire; verified empirically rather than trusted from GSOD docs.
  - **Caught by adversarial pre-merge review on PR #3, before the branch merged — not CI.** PR #3 has zero GitHub review threads; the catch is documented only in the PR body/commit messages.
  - **A guard adjacent to the bug it can't catch:** the `accepted_range(450, 1090)` test looks like it protects this column but structurally cannot — 999.9 sits *inside* [450, 1090], so even if the test ran it would pass on the missing marker. And it never runs in CI (dbt parse opens no connection; severity: warn regardless). The guardrail is deliberately wider than the observed 706-1055 mb to catch regressions, not to fit the current distribution.
  - **Later context (don't overstate current impact):** since the 2026-07 hourly rebuild, the decoded GSOD pressure column's role is largely the *station registry* behind `airport_station_map`; ML origin weather now comes from ISD hourly (last obs at/before scheduled departure). But at PR #3 the pressure column was a real ~45%-wrong data defect in a consumed feature.
  - Header comment literally annotates `13.5 = 1013.5 mb`; a vivid "public reference data lies about itself" anecdote — the data contradicts its own documentation. Cross-ref ch 10 (review-not-CI), ch 16 (know-your-data / raw-in-silver discipline).

### Chapter 14 - The overlapping-interval catch (223k phantom successors; failing them IMPROVED every metric)
- **Numbers / evidence:** **341k** same-tail overlapping scheduled intervals; **2/3 overlap by >30 min** (phantom/duplicate BTS records, not rounding); their **223k successor rows** would have used them as "valid" inbounds. Fail-closing them retrained *up* on **every** metric: ROC **0.7339 → 0.7397**, PR-AUC **0.4624 → 0.4748** (also RMSE 49.56, MAE 19.00). Cleaned turnaround-band feature rose to **#2** importance; operated-chain rotation_position fell **#4 → #8**. Overlap threshold 30 min; tail-unknown legs 0.34%; row set 20,240,662; split 16,678,880 / 3,561,782.
- **Why it's strong:** A data-quality fix that made the model *better* — the exact opposite of the usual fix-costs-accuracy tradeoff — and the metric jump is itself the evidence the removed edges were corrupt.
- **Narrative beat:** Removing bad edges beat keeping more data. Refusing to treat overlapped/duplicate tail records as valid inbounds sharpened the signal; the improvement is used as proof the linkage was real contamination, not noise.
- **Source:** PR #16 (Codex packaging review, commit 6fc41d0); code area rotation_model.
- **Details:**
  - **The bug:** same-tail legs whose scheduled intervals overlap (an aircraft "in two places at once" — phantom/duplicate records) could serve as inbounds for their successors, polluting turnaround and inbound features on 223k rows.
  - **The fix (fail-closed):** an `overlap_flagged` CTE sets `prior_leg_overlapped` via `lag(raw_gap_min < 0)`; a successor of an overlapped leg takes the no-inbound path and is excluded from the consistent-inbound class. Mirrored in the standing schedule-only guard.
  - **Caught by Codex packaging review, not CI.** dbt-parse/ruff/gitleaks/dagster-validate all pass a mart with corrupted inbound linkage; only reading the join logic surfaced it.
  - **Adjacent catch, same model:** one leg in 20.2M had a NULL flight_number silently dropped by the equality join between the mart and the rotation model (would have produced a NULL `has_inbound_leg`); the guard's own `rotation_join_miss` arm caught it; fix is a null-safe join (`IS NOT DISTINCT FROM`) everywhere the natural key is used (self/author, medium).
  - **Mechanism confirmation:** the no_inbound turnaround-band training rate fell (0.388 → 0.224 once swap-shaped rows also left it — PR #18), and the retrained model reorganized exactly as a real-signal hypothesis predicts (cleaned turnaround-band up to #2, operated-chain rotation_position down #4→#8), evidence the metric gain is signal not overf-fit.

### Chapter 15 - Test-scoping philosophy (scope the guard to the rows that can break)
- **Numbers / evidence:** **118 of 1,274** seed rows carry no timezone (verified: airports.csv has 1,274 data rows, exactly 118 with empty tz). **All 375 flown airports carry tz** (`assert_flown_airports_have_timezone` → PASS on current data). PR #4: 26 additions, 1 file, the SQL is 26 lines; merged ~5m29s after opening, 0 reviewers/comments. Negative control: BIH tz simulated to NULL → compiled SQL returns `'BIH'` (fails with 1 row). BIH (Eastern Sierra Regional, Bishop CA): **1,712 BTS legs 2022-2024**, OpenFlights ships tz=`\N`, backfilled to `America/Los_Angeles`. TZ_BACKFILL is a single entry (1 airport). The relationships tests on stg_bts_flights.origin/dest pin all **375** BTS codes.
- **Why it's strong:** "A data-quality test should assert only over the rows where a failure could actually cause harm" — with a live example (118 harmless rows) of how the over-broad version trains reviewers to ignore the alert.
- **Narrative beat:** The obvious move (blanket `not_null` on the seed's tz column) would be perpetually red on 118 harmless general-aviation strips no flight touches — a test that cries wolf gets muted. Scoping to the FLOWN subset makes it green today and bite precisely when a flown airport loses its tz.
- **Source:** PR #4 (feat/tz guard); PR #3 (BIH backfill); PR #1 (SUPPLEMENTS precedent).
- **Details:**
  - **The scoped test:** `assert_flown_airports_have_timezone` intersects the distinct origin/dest of stg_bts_flights (the "flown" set) with the airports seed and returns one row per offending IATA where `tz IS NULL OR trim(tz)=''` — a *singular* custom-SQL test because the predicate (intersect-with-flown, then flag NULL-or-empty) can't be a stock `not_null`; the offending codes become the failure rows (actionable, not just a count).
  - **Why blanket not_null was rejected:** it would fail all 118 tz-less seed rows today (small GA fields OpenFlights ships without a tz, referenced by no BTS flight) — a permanent false alarm; "a tz-less airport that no flight references cannot break anything; the moment one becomes flown, this test fires."
  - **Composable guards over one omnibus test:** two orthogonal failure modes — flown-airport-present-but-tz-missing (this test) vs flown-airport-absent-from-seed (the existing relationships tests pinning all 375 codes) — each with a clean scope. The inner join here deliberately does NOT re-cover the missing-row case.
  - **Falsification / negative-control discipline:** proving the guard PASSes is only half the evidence; the author re-ran the compiled SQL with BIH's tz simulated to NULL to prove it returns `'BIH'` and would fail with 1 row — proving the test *can* bite.
  - **The BIH single-airport hardcode debate ("hardcode vs documented override"):** the response chain is catch (PR #3 tz not_null test) → backfill (single-entry `TZ_BACKFILL` map, BIH → America/Los_Angeles) → institutionalize (PR #4 standing guard). The hardcode is defensible because it's a *documented override that defers to upstream* — "upstream wins" precedence: if OpenFlights later ships a tz for BIH it takes over (logs the change). Justified precisely because "silver tests pin tz not_null for flown airports" — a NULL tz would break local-time features. Same "manual gap-fill that refuses to rot" pattern as PR #1's `SUPPLEMENTS` (XWA/EAR/IFP, upstream-wins).
  - **Deliberate restraint:** the seed is kept byte-identical to main; the single TZ_BACKFILL entry is left un-expanded — do the minimum that closes the real gap, don't pre-emptively backfill 118 harmless rows.
  - Defensive one-sided normalization: the join normalizes the seed side (`upper(trim(airports.iata))`) to match already-cleaned stg codes, hardening against casing/whitespace drift. Caught/authored pre-merge; not CI. Cross-ref ch 10 (a test is the artifact that outlives the incident).

### Chapter 16 - Know-your-data at serve time (the visibility instrumentation artifact)
- **Numbers / evidence:** **0.16%** of ISD observations exceed the nominal 10-mi cap (16093 m), **max observed 99.4 mi** — instrumentation heterogeneity (extended-reporting software), not weather. Silver keeps RAW visibility; the ML mart right-censors uniformly at **10.0** via `least(visibility_mi, 10.0)` ("10 or better"). VIS conversion: metres / 1609.344 → statute miles; sentinel 999999 = missing; QC element-null fraction VIS **0.51%**. `accepted_range`: silver visibility_mi 0-100 (warn); mart origin_visibility_mi 0-10.001 (warn). At serve time, absent NDFD visibility at a resolved grid hour maps to **10.0 (clear)**, not NaN — the fix moved a clear-day ORD score **0.8069 → 0.7824** (PR #15).
- **Why it's strong:** A subtle training/serving distribution-alignment call — the "right" default at serve time is dictated by *why* the sensor is silent, not by treating absence as missing.
- **Narrative beat:** A sensor quirk (0.16% of readings up to 99.4 mi) is not weather signal — so censor it. Then at serve time the mirror-image subtlety: NDFD publishes visibility *mainly during restriction*, so a clear-sky hour returns *no* value — and mapping that to NaN would be a serving-only pattern training almost never produced. Absent-at-a-resolved-hour must mean clear (10.0), the mart's censored clear-day value.
- **Source:** PR #14 (silver decode + mart censoring), PR #15 (serving clear-sky mapping); code areas silver_weather, serving_edges.
- **Details:**
  - **The artifact:** whether a station caps at 10 mi is instrumentation heterogeneity; passing raw values (up to 99.4 mi) to the model injects a sensor artifact rather than signal. "Don't let an instrument's reporting style become spurious signal."
  - **Clean medallion boundary:** the artifact is preserved RAW in silver (auditable, honest) and censored only at the ML mart, where the modeling decision belongs — never mutating the source of record.
  - **Serve-time alignment (PR #15, self, medium):** leaving visibility NaN at a resolved clear-sky hour is a serving-only pattern (training rows with an observation almost never carry NaN visibility). Fix: absent visibility at a resolved grid hour → 10.0, matching the mart's right-censored clear value — the "NDFD absence = unrestricted = clear" reasoning. **Surfaced by the live end-to-end test itself** (the clear-day ORD score moving 0.8069 → 0.7824), a rare case of serving-time integration testing *changing a design decision mid-PR*, catching a train/serve skew unit-level reasoning had missed.
  - **Sibling know-your-data decisions in the same decoder (color/backdrop):** structural-zero vs missing in precip — no AA groups at all → 0.0 (METAR "nothing to report"); AA groups present but only longer-period synoptic totals / a failed-QC 1-hr group → NULL (honest missingness); only the period-01 group feeds `precip_1h_in`, accumulation windows never mixed. Gust is NULL-when-unreported with a `gust_reported` indicator so downstream can encode absent-as-calm (0.0 + flag) without losing the reported/absent signal. Two different missingness semantics decoded from the same style of packed ISD field.
  - **QC policy:** element quality codes 2/3/6/7 null the ELEMENT not the row; affected fractions reported at build time (TMP 0.21%, DEW 0.14%, WND 0.015%, VIS 0.51%, gust 1.86%, AA1 0.13%) — visible data-quality accounting baked into the decoder.
  - Faithfulness-to-distribution theme shared with ch 13 (the 337-min parable); censoring/serving-alignment caught by know-your-data design + the live test, not CI. Cross-ref ch 11 (raw-in-silver discipline), ch 12 (encoding judgment calls).

- **Details:**
  - No-duplication is enforced by **graph topology, not convention**: `int_historical_delay_rates` fans both ways — → `mart_delays_by_{route,carrier,airport}` → `dash_*` views (Streamlit/Looker dashboard) AND → `ml_flight_features` (ML mart). Header: "Both the analytics marts and the ML feature mart must ref() this model; nothing recomputes these rates." `int_aircraft_rotation` likewise `ref()`'d exactly once by the ML mart and the rates model.

"Change it HERE only — no model may inline a cutoff literal." One WHERE clause is the entire train/test firewall for both consumption models.

  - Review vs CI: the drift-proof is a harness (`verify.py`) + byte-exact metrics, not CI; a linter can't see whether two consumers compute the same rate.

### Chapter 25 - Two-regime framing: pre-departure/ticketing vs real-time operational
- **Numbers / evidence:** Built model = **pre-departure/ticketing regime**, held-out headline **ROC 0.7389 / PR-AUC 0.4652** (pinned `0.7388902208 / 0.4651540494`), tuned regressor RMSE 49.26 / MAE 18.99 (`49.264351 / 18.985849`). Feature-generation progression (all strictly pre-departure): daily weather `0.6806 / 0.3479` → hourly-at-departure `0.6979 / 0.3893` → cascade **contaminated** `0.7397 / 0.4748` → swap-restricted **production** `0.7389 / 0.4652`. **The regime boundary, quantified:** removing the swap-linkage leak (an operational, day-of signal) costs PR-AUC `0.0096` (`0.4748 - 0.4652`, ≈ **11%** of the +0.0855 cascade uplift); **89% survived** (`+0.0759 of +0.0855`); ROC keeps 98% of its cascade gain. no_inbound band training delay rate `0.388 → 0.224` once swap-shaped rows leave (the elevated rate was substantially an operational-disruption fingerprint). Schedule-consistency split: 91.95% consistent inbound / 3.93% clean first leg / **4.12% swap-shaped → NULL**; swap-shape overrepresented in evenings (4.85% evening vs 4.12% overall; evening classes a/b/c = 92.65% / 2.50% / 4.85%). **Error ceiling = delay unknowable in advance:** regression tail-dominated, MAE ~13 min (on-time) rising to **~315 min for 3h+ delays** (daily era); cascade era 60-180 min MAE `76.8 → 68.7`, 180+ min MAE `310.8 → 281.0`. Restriction made regression *slightly worse* (RMSE/MAE `49.71/19.10` restricted vs `49.56/19.00` contaminated) and was shipped/reported anyway. Morning vs evening lift (restricted shipping model): `2.56x / 1.95x`; dashboard two-regime echo — "Summer drives delays (**25.1%**), winter drives cancellations (**2.77%**) — two different operational failure modes."
- **Why it's strong:** "0.7389 isn't a weak model — it's the honest ceiling of the *hard* regime; a real-time operational model with legal actual-progress features would be easier and score higher, and I can quantify the boundary between them."
- **Narrative beat:** The gap between what was built (pre-departure) and a hypothetical operational model *is* the point — it's the quantity of delay that is simply unknowable before departure. The cascade "contaminated" model (0.7397) was a partial peek into the operational regime via the post-hoc operated-tail linkage; measuring the peek (0.0096 PR-AUC, ~11%) and *refusing to ship it* turns a leakage caveat into an honest, priced choice.
- **Source:** PR #18 (tail-swap restriction), PR #16 (cascade), PR #10 (error ceiling), PR #14 (morning/evening mechanism), PR #15 (leak-free-by-construction serving), PR #17 (serving reconciliation), PR #5 (prior-day = leakage-safe stand-in), code: architecture_docs, code: rotation_model, code: serving_edges.
- **Details:**
  - **The boundary is CLAUDE.md §9's leakage rule.** Regime 1 (pre-departure/ticketing) may use only info knowable before departure. Regime 2 (real-time operational) features are the forbidden set: `DepDelay`, `ArrDelay`, `ArrDelayMinutes`, actual gate/wheels times, diverted/cancelled outcomes, `ArrDel15` (for the classifier's features), and the true operated-tail cascade linkage. An operational model may legally use these actual-progress signals — a *different, easier, higher-AUC* model.
  - **Boundary extended from VALUES to LINKAGE (PR #18, the flagship two-regime turn):** rotation features chain legs by the post-hoc OPERATED tail; a swap-restructured linkage is itself a day-of operational outcome. So rotation features exist only for schedule-consistent links; swap-shaped (4.12%) → NULL. This *resolved* a limitation PR #16 had only disclosed as an "epistemology caveat" (the block was deleted from the mart header and replaced by the restriction).
  - **Mechanism verified, not assumed:** no_inbound band rate `0.388 → 0.224` (swap fingerprint removed); retrained model reorganized as a real-signal hypothesis predicts — cleaned turnaround-band rose to #2 importance while operated-chain rotation_position (which absorbs swap restructuring) fell #4 → #8. 89% of the uplift was genuine pre-departure schedule structure, not operational leakage.
  - **Analytics keeps the operational regime on purpose:** `fact_flights` deliberately retains dep_delay, arr_del15, cancelled, diverted, actual times as measures — the leakage boundary is the ML mart, *not* analytics (CLAUDE.md §4). Two independent consumption models, one gold layer (see ch 19).
  - **Error-ceiling framing = the unknowable quantity:** extreme delays (3h+, MAE ~315 min) are day-of shocks no pre-departure feature can see; the ceiling is acknowledged as irreducible for this regime rather than papered over. RMSE barely beats the mean predictor because RMSE is tail-dominated (see ch 5).
  - **Morning/evening mechanism (PR #14 → refuted pre-registered hypothesis, ch 1):** morning delays are weather-determined (little overnight cascade accumulated — the pre-departure regime *can* see these), evening delays are cascade/operational-determined; features pay off where exposure VARIES (mornings span first-leg-to-red-eye), not where its effects dominate (by evening every tail is uniformly deep in rotation). Evening overrepresentation of swap-shape (4.85%) independently confirms the operational-accumulation pattern.
  - **Serving reconciliation — the cleanest pre-departure-vs-operational statement (PR #17/#18):** under the restriction, all-NULL rotation is now *in-distribution* but MEANS "operated linkage was swap-restructured" (a day-of/operational outcome). At serve time a caller who merely doesn't know a future flight's rotation must be given the TYPICAL rotation profile (training medians), NOT NULL — because NULL would misclassify a *merely-unknown future plan* as *swap-shaped operational disruption*. Same code, semantically deeper reason (it originally read as a distributional/OOD argument, then flipped to this semantic one).
  - **Serving is leak-free by construction (PR #15):** a forecast issued *now* for a flight that has not departed predates departure by construction, so it is legitimately pre-departure info; FlightRequest carries schedule fields only. Scoring an *already-departed* flight uses the current grid (which may postdate departure) and is explicitly logged as "not a pre-departure prediction" — protecting the regime boundary even in the debug path.
  - **Prior-day weather as the leakage-safe stand-in (PR #5):** the mart header frames prior-day GSOD obs as the leakage-safe stand-in for a production "forecast-issued-at-T" feed — an explicit two-regime nod: production would use a forecast, the historically-honest proxy is the prior day. PR #14's hourly-at-departure weather collapses two train/serve mismatches into one (forecast-vs-observed), and that remaining gap doesn't touch the held-out metrics (computed on observed data).
  - **Dashboard echo:** "Summer drives delays (25.1%), winter drives cancellations (2.77%) — two different operational failure modes" — a descriptive two-regime hook separating weather-delay from weather-cancellation seasonality.
  - **Calibration ties in (ch 24):** the pre-departure classifier's recall-weighted scores are remapped to honest frequencies without disturbing ranking — probability honesty *within* the built regime.
  - **Honest linear-model footnote:** logreg retains almost none of the cascade uplift under the restriction (`0.3382` vs hourly `0.3310`) because it median-imputes the 4.12% all-NULL rows and loses the band separation; the surviving signal is largely tree-accessible (XGBoost consumes NULLs natively). A clean illustration that a leakage/regime-boundary fix can be near-invisible to a linear model and material to trees.

### Chapter 26 - The second benchmark, and the nondeterminism it exposed
- **Numbers / evidence:** Per `/predict` call, executed-job statistics with cache off and `cache_hit` asserted false, 3 reps: **6 BigQuery queries → 0**, **2,311,725,056 bytes (2.31 GB) → 0**, **$0.01315 → $0** (on-demand $6.25/TiB), **$1,315 → $0 per 100,000 predictions**. Median latency 1 flight **5,078 ms → 13 ms (390x)**; 1,500 flights **11,207 ms → 194 ms (58x)**. Startup billed **3,028,287,488 B (3.03 GB) → 41,943,040 B (42 MB), 72x** — but startup WALL CLOCK **7,871 ms → 7,675 ms, ~unchanged** (dominated by loading ~730 MB of artifacts, not queries). Live: the ORD batch demo went **8.3 s → 1.8 s** for ~840 flights. The replacement is three dbt tables: `serving_entity_profile` **8,316 rows**, `serving_density_profile` **34,979 rows**, `serving_typical_rotation` **1 row**. The old cost was FLAT in batch size — a 1-flight and a 1,500-flight request both scanned 2.31 GB — because none of the five lookups carried a `flight_date` predicate, so partition pruning never applied over the 20,240,662-row mart.
- **Why it's strong:** A second, independent before/after on a different axis from the 33.8x partition benchmark — that one is analytics scan volume, this one is the inference path — and it ends in a *correctness* find rather than a speed number.
- **Narrative beat:** The optimization was routine (materialize constants that only change on a dbt rebuild). What made it worth writing about is what fell out of checking parity: **the thing being optimized was already nondeterministic, and nobody knew.**
- **Source:** PR #26; docs/benchmarks/serving_preload_benchmark.md; ml/parity.py; dbt/models/gold/ml/serving_*.sql.
- **Details:**
  - **The find.** The "typical rotation profile" — training medians of the rotation schedule attributes, used by every prediction made WITHOUT rotation context, i.e. every consumer request — was computed with `approx_quantiles(x, 2)[offset(1)]` **at process startup**. That is an approximation whose result depends on how BigQuery shards the scan. The same query on identical data was observed returning **four different values for `inbound_distance`: 666 / 674 / 663 / 651**, against an exact median of **667**; `sched_turnaround_min` came back **63** against an exact **64**. So two processes serving the same request could return different probabilities. Largest divergence measured in the golden set: a flight moving **0.4300 → 0.4968** — a 6.7-point swing attributable entirely to approximation noise in a lookup.
  - **The second, smaller instance of the same class:** `any_value(distance)` per route. **85 of 7,539 routes carry two distinct distances** (a 1-mile rounding split in the BTS source), so `any_value` picked arbitrarily per call. Fixed with `min(distance)` — deterministic, and 1 mile is immaterial to a feature spanning ~30–5,000.
  - **Both fixes are `percentile_disc` / `min` — exact and deterministic. Verified by rebuilding the three tables twice and diffing: byte-identical.** Costs ~35 s once per dbt build instead of an approximation per startup.
  - **Parity had to be reported in two parts, and that is the honest bit.** The refactor was supposed to be bit-identical, but the determinism fix deliberately changes values. So: 184 golden requests spanning both rotation paths, both density paths, four entities absent from the training vocabulary. **28 of 28 requests that supplied both rotation context and density are bit-identical** across all 51 features, the calibrated probability and the expected minutes. Of the 156 that depend on the changed medians, **151 were unchanged anyway**, and the only features that moved across the entire run were the four those medians feed (`inbound_distance`, `sched_turnaround_min`, `sched_turnaround_slack_min`, `origin_dep_density_hour`). Mean |Δp| **0.00049**, max **0.0668**.
  - **The harness itself had the bug it was written to catch — twice over, found by an automated reviewer AFTER merge.** v1 sampled its flights with `any_value()` per column over a `(carrier, origin, dest, dep_time)` group. A group spans many dates, so each column could come from a DIFFERENT flight — assembling rotation contexts no real flight has — and the arbitrary picks could differ between two captures of identical code, producing FALSE parity regressions. Fixed with `row_number()` over a total order, taking every field from one row; two captures of identical code are now bit-identical. Second finding, sharper: `compare()` only failed on the independent subset, so a broken density table or typical-profile lookup — the exact behaviour the change introduced — would land entirely in the "informational" half and **exit 0**. It now fails by default on any drift, and under `--expect-medians-change` still fails if a request that supplied its own context moved or if a moved feature is one the medians cannot reach. Verified against doctored fixtures in all three directions. The lesson worth writing: *a parity harness is itself code, and mine reproduced the exact class of defect it existed to detect.*
  - **A methodology round, exactly like the 33.8x benchmark had three (ch. 21).** The first baseline measurement was WRONG: reps 2–3 at n=100 and n=1,500 reported **0 bytes** because the flight sample's key set saturated, the SQL repeated verbatim, and BigQuery's 24-hour result cache served it. That would have understated the baseline by ~2.3 GB per call. Fixed by forcing `use_query_cache=False` and asserting `job.cache_hit` is false. Same failure mode as the dry-run-estimates round in ch. 21: the first number you get from a benchmark harness is usually measuring the harness.
  - **The startup wall clock is reported as unchanged, deliberately.** 7.9 s → 7.7 s. The 72x is billed bytes, not speed — artifact loading dominates. Quoting only the 72x would be the misleading version of this result.
  - **Review discipline, again (ch. 10).** A code review of the PR found 14 issues; the load-bearing three: the new `include_features=False` flag was **dead** (the one bulk caller still built the 51-key block and popped it, so the stated optimization was unrealized); an undeclared ordering contract between two loader functions meant re-running one erased the typical profile and every context-less request would then score on an out-of-distribution NaN vector **instead of raising**; and `build_context` hit a bare BigQuery 404 on the common deploy mistake (new image, old dataset) instead of the actionable error the code carefully raises for the empty case.
  - **The best small story in the whole PR.** The review's point that the golden-vector harness belonged in the repo — the benchmark's correctness claim rested on it, and a SQL header instructed future editors to "re-run the golden-vector parity check" against a file that was not committed. Committing it as `ml/parity.py` and re-running it **immediately caught a bug introduced while fixing the review**: the loader's return type was changed to `dict` and the `return` statement forgotten, so `build_context` died on `NoneType` subscript. Ruff does not catch that and mypy is not in CI. The harness paid for itself within minutes of being committed.
  - **New standing guards:** `assert_serving_lookup_entities_constant` pins the constant-within-entity property that every `any_value()` in the lookup model depends on — including the re-derived `turnaround_band` / `rotation_position` keys, so a threshold moving on one side of that SQL/Python mirror fails the build instead of making `any_value` arbitrary. Plus `assert_serving_typical_rotation_singleton` (serving pins every context-less prediction to `med[0]`) and 16 pure unit tests with no credentials.
  - **Cross-refs:** ch. 21 (the other benchmark, and its methodology rounds), ch. 9 (across-rebuild determinism — this is the same value applied to the serving tier), ch. 10 (review discipline), ch. 22 (artifact/contract thinking), ch. 17 (the lookups are a fourth purpose-built shape off the one gold layer).
